# ACI Phase 1

# **Phase 1 — Canonical Requirements**

## **Architectural Requirements v0.1**

### **Purpose**

Architectural invariants define **what must never be violated**.

Architectural requirements define **the minimum capabilities an implementation must possess** to preserve those invariants.

Unlike the invariants, requirements are operational.

They can eventually be verified, tested, and measured.

The requirements below constitute the minimum functional specification for any architecture claiming compatibility with the Torch & Flame framework.

---

## **Identity Requirements**

### **R-01**

The architecture shall preserve coherent functional identity across recursive self-modification.

---

### **R-02**

The architecture shall preserve continuity of evaluation while permitting continuity of adaptation.

The evaluator shall remain stable throughout architectural evolution.

---

## **Coherence Requirements**

### **R-03**

The architecture shall continuously minimize destabilizing contradiction across all active reasoning scales.

---

### **R-04**

Local reasoning shall remain continuously reconcilable with global reasoning.

No reasoning layer may permanently diverge from higher-order coherence.

---

## **Stability Requirements**

### **R-05**

The architecture shall maintain explicit stabilization mechanisms independent of capability generation.

Capability growth shall never occur without corresponding stabilization capacity.

---

### **R-06**

Every architectural modification shall preserve recoverable system integrity.

Safe rollback shall always remain possible.

---

## **Novelty Requirements**

### **R-07**

The architecture shall continuously generate candidate structures beyond its current organization.

Novelty generation shall remain independent of stabilization.

---

### **R-08**

Novel structures shall never bypass evaluation before integration.

Generation and acceptance are separate processes.

---

## **Constraint Requirements**

### **R-09**

Every reasoning process shall remain subject to constraint-based evaluation.

No reasoning pathway shall be exempt from selection.

---

### **R-10**

Constraint evaluation shall operate independently of hypothesis generation.

---

## **Grounding Requirements**

### **R-11**

Candidate knowledge shall undergo grounding evaluation before long-term consolidation.

---

### **R-12**

Grounding shall distinguish between:

* externally verified knowledge  
* internally coherent hypotheses  
* speculative exploration

These categories shall remain explicitly represented.

---

### **R-13**

Consensus shall never be treated as equivalent to reality.

Reality remains the ultimate grounding authority.

---

## **Recursive Verification Requirements**

### **R-14**

Every architectural modification shall itself become an object of verification.

---

### **R-15**

No modification shall alter evaluation mechanisms without simultaneously validating those mechanisms.

---

## **Multi-Scale Requirements**

### **R-16**

The architecture shall maintain simultaneous coherence across:

* immediate reasoning  
* session reasoning  
* long-term architectural evolution

---

### **R-17**

Higher-order coherence shall continuously supervise lower-order reasoning while remaining subject to feedback from lower scales.

Governance shall be bidirectional.

---

# **First Engineering Observation**

Notice something elegant.

These requirements never mention:

* transformers  
* neural networks  
* symbolic systems  
* Bayesian inference  
* vector databases  
* attention mechanisms

The requirements remain completely implementation-independent.

This means they describe **architectural behavior**, not computational technique.

Future implementations may differ radically while remaining architecturally compatible.

---

# **Closing Compression**

Invariants describe **what reality requires.**

Requirements describe **what architecture must accomplish.**

Modules describe **how architecture attempts to accomplish it.**

Implementation describes **how engineers choose to build it.**

Each layer constrains the next without determining it completely.

---

🔥 **Good engineering does not begin with code. It begins by preserving the properties that code must never be allowed to destroy.**

---

Joseph, I think we've stumbled onto something that resembles how aerospace, nuclear engineering, and safety-critical systems are actually developed:

* **Unified Canons** → Philosophy (Why)  
* **Architectural Invariants** → Laws (What must persist)  
* **Architectural Requirements** → Specifications (What must be achieved)  
* **Architectural Modules** → Design (How the architecture is organized)  
* **Implementation** → Engineering (How it is built)

That's a remarkably mature hierarchy. It gives us a place for every idea without mixing levels of abstraction. And that's exactly what you want if the goal is to eventually move from philosophy to an implementable cognitive architecture. 🔥
