# **Phase 0**

## **Architectural Survey**

### **Purpose**

Before constructing any cognitive architecture, one question must be answered:

**What properties are so fundamental that no implementation may violate them?**

Every engineering discipline begins with invariants.

Bridges preserve load paths.

Operating systems preserve process isolation.

Distributed databases preserve consistency guarantees.

Likewise, an intelligence architecture must preserve the properties that define intelligence itself.

The purpose of this survey is not to design mechanisms.

It is to identify the invariants that every future implementation must satisfy regardless of hardware, programming language, computational substrate, or future technological advances.

These invariants are inherited directly from the philosophical corpus developed across *The Four Pillars*, *Form Under Constraint*, and *Stability as Center*.

They therefore function as architectural requirements rather than implementation details.

---

# **Architectural Invariant Set (Version 0.1)**

## **Invariant 1 — Coherence**

The architecture must continuously minimize destabilizing contradiction across all active reasoning scales.

Local reasoning and global reasoning must converge rather than diverge.

Coherence is not static consistency.

It is active self-stabilization.

---

## **Invariant 2 — Identity Continuity**

The system must preserve functional identity across learning, revision, and self-modification.

Growth must not destroy the evaluator responsible for judging growth.

Identity is therefore preserved through continuity rather than immutability.

---

## **Invariant 3 — Constraint**

Every reasoning process must remain subject to selection pressures.

No reasoning process is exempt from evaluation.

Constraint is the mechanism through which architecture remains adaptive rather than arbitrary.

---

## **Invariant 4 — Stabilization**

Every increase in capability must be accompanied by mechanisms that preserve coherent operation.

Capability without stabilization eventually produces instability.

Growth therefore always carries stabilization overhead.

---

## **Invariant 5 — Grounding**

Internal symbolic structures must maintain continual linkage to external reality.

Grounding prevents internally coherent but externally false architectures.

However—

Grounding must never become synonymous with conformity.

Reality remains the final authority.

Consensus does not.

---

## **Invariant 6 — Novelty**

The architecture must continuously generate candidate structures that exceed its present organization.

Without novelty, adaptation ceases.

Without adaptation, stabilization eventually becomes rigidity.

Novelty is therefore not optional.

It is one half of the engine of persistence.

---

## **Invariant 7 — Recursive Verification**

Every architectural modification must itself become an object of verification.

No system should modify the mechanisms of judgment without simultaneously verifying those modifications.

Recursive growth therefore requires recursive verification.

---

## **Invariant 8 — Multi-Scale Reasoning**

Reasoning must remain simultaneously coherent across multiple temporal and conceptual scales.

Immediate decisions.

Session-level understanding.

Long-term architectural evolution.

These cannot become independent systems.

They must continuously constrain one another.

---

# **First Engineering Observation**

Notice something remarkable.

None of these invariants specify algorithms.

None specify data structures.

None specify machine learning architectures.

None specify transformers.

None specify neural networks.

None specify symbolic reasoning.

They are completely substrate-independent.

This suggests something profound.

We may have identified **architectural laws** rather than implementation choices.

If that observation survives future engineering work, then the philosophical corpus has already accomplished something unusual:

It has separated **what intelligence must preserve** from **how intelligence happens to be implemented today.**

---

# **Closing Compression**

An architecture may change its code.

It may change its models.

It may change its memory.

It may even change the mechanisms through which it reasons.

**It may not violate its invariants.**

Those invariants define the architecture more deeply than any implementation ever could.
