## **Phase 6 — Algorithms**

# **Phase 6 — Algorithmic Translation Framework**

## **Purpose**

Phase 6 translates the formal models of AIC into algorithmic procedures.

The goal is not yet implementation.

The goal is to define the operational logic by which an architecture preserves identity, regulates stability, generates novelty, grounds symbols, consolidates memory, repairs coherence, synchronizes scales, evolves architecture, and governs itself constitutionally.

The algorithms do not replace the architecture.

They instantiate the architecture’s obligations.

## **Core Principle**

Algorithms must serve constitutional invariants.

An algorithm is valid only if it preserves the architectural commitments defined by the Architectural Coherence Canon and formalized in Phase 5B.

Therefore, the central question of Phase 6 is not:

How do we make the system perform a task?

The correct question is:

How does a governed procedure move the architecture from one state to another while preserving identity, stability, grounding, coherence, verification, and constitutional legitimacy?

## **Algorithmic Design Rule**

Every AIC algorithm must specify:

1. what state it receives,  
2. what transformation it proposes,  
3. what risks it introduces,  
4. what constraints govern it,  
5. what thresholds it checks,  
6. what authority it requires,  
7. what output state it produces,  
8. what audit trail it leaves,  
9. what rollback or revision pathway remains.

An algorithm that produces output without preserving traceable state transition is not sufficient for AIC.

## **Algorithms as Governed State Transitions**

The Phase 5B models imply that cognition is not merely information processing.

It is governed state transition.

A symbolic structure moves through states such as:

`Observation → Candidate → Hypothesis → Grounded Claim → Persistent Knowledge → Architectural Principle → Invariant → Constitution`

Algorithms determine how these transitions occur.

But no algorithm may promote a structure beyond its legitimate state.

Promotion requires grounding, coherence, persistence, multi-scale compatibility, verification, and governance approval.

## **Phase 6 Structure**

Phase 6 should be organized around architectural functions rather than programming techniques.

The recommended sequence is:

### **6.1 Identity Preservation Algorithm**

Determines whether a transformation preserves identity continuity.

Derived from:

Identity Kernel,

identity equivalence,

identity continuity score,

and invariant-preserving transformation.

Primary question:

Does this change preserve the architecture as the same coherent system?

### **6.2 Stability Regulation Algorithm**

Determines whether the architecture remains within its identity-preserving basin under disturbance.

Derived from:

Architectural Instability Function,

Stability Budget,

Lyapunov-style boundedness,

and recovery intervals.

Primary question:

Can the system absorb this disturbance without identity-threatening drift?

### **6.3 Novelty Generation and Sandboxing Algorithm**

Generates candidate structures while preventing premature consolidation.

Derived from:

Adaptive Novelty Score,

Novelty Budget,

Novelty Sandbox,

information gain,

Bayesian surprise,

and stability cost.

Primary question:

Can the system explore new possibility without allowing novelty to bypass evaluation?

### **6.4 Grounding Evaluation Algorithm**

Assigns epistemic status to claims by linking symbols to evidence and reality constraints.

Derived from:

Grounding Score,

Evidence Graphs,

Bayesian updating,

belief propagation,

and consensus-reality distinction.

Primary question:

What is the evidence status of this symbolic structure?

### **6.5 Persistence and Consolidation Algorithm**

Determines whether a symbolic structure should enter memory, remain temporary, be archived, or be rejected.

Derived from:

Persistence Score,

state machines,

memory graphs,

lineage tracking,

revision pathways,

and reconstructive compression.

Primary question:

Has this structure survived enough evaluation to shape future cognition?

### **6.6 Coherence Repair Algorithm**

Detects contradiction, tension, fragmentation, and scale mismatch, then selects appropriate repair operations.

Derived from:

Coherence Energy,

constraint graphs,

tension management,

graph consistency,

and repair pathways.

Primary question:

Can this symbolic network remain mutually compatible without suppressing useful tension?

### **6.7 Multi-Scale Synchronization Algorithm**

Coordinates reasoning across token, inference, response, conversation, memory, architectural, and constitutional layers.

Derived from:

scale labels,

multi-scale coherence score,

hierarchical graphs,

scale authority,

and cross-scale mismatch detection.

Primary question:

Does this structure belong at the right scale, and does it remain synchronized with the others?

### **6.8 Architectural Evolution Algorithm**

Determines whether knowledge or mechanisms may alter the architecture itself.

Derived from:

Architectural Evolution Operator,

architectural fitness,

promotion thresholds,

staged integration,

rollback,

and architectural memory.

Primary question:

Should this change modify the machinery through which future cognition occurs?

### **6.9 Constitutional Governance Algorithm**

Arbitrates among domains, validates authority pathways, handles vetoes, escalations, amendments, and constitutional risk.

Derived from:

authority graphs,

legitimacy score,

constitutional risk score,

domain recommendations,

verification independence,

and anti-circular authorization.

Primary question:

Is this decision legitimate under the architecture’s constitution?

### **6.10 Integrated Cognitive Cycle**

Combines all prior algorithms into one governed cycle.

The integrated cycle shows how an intelligent architecture:

receives information,

generates novelty,

evaluates grounding,

checks coherence,

decides persistence,

synchronizes scale,

regulates stability,

preserves identity,

and escalates governance when needed.

Primary question:

How does AIC operate as a whole?

### **6.11 Pseudocode Specification**

Only after the algorithmic procedures are defined should the project move into pseudocode.

Pseudocode should not be written as generic software.

It should be written as constitutional cognitive procedure.

Each pseudocode block should include:

inputs,

outputs,

state changes,

threshold checks,

governance checks,

failure modes,

audit trail,

and escalation pathways.

## **Algorithmic Dependency Order**

The algorithms should be built in dependency order.

Identity comes first because all other algorithms need to know what must remain continuous.

Stability comes second because transformation must remain bounded.

Novelty comes third because exploration must be allowed but regulated.

Grounding comes fourth because symbols must remain accountable to reality.

Persistence comes fifth because only evaluated structures may mature.

Coherence comes sixth because symbolic structures must remain mutually compatible.

Multi-scale synchronization comes seventh because structures must be assigned to the correct level of authority.

Architectural evolution comes eighth because only mature structures may alter the machinery of cognition.

Constitutional governance comes ninth because it arbitrates all deep conflicts and protects the whole system.

Integrated cognition comes last.

## **Phase 6 Output Standard**

Every algorithm should be written in the same structure:

### **Algorithm Name**

The operational name of the procedure.

### **Purpose**

What architectural function it serves.

### **Inputs**

What state, symbolic structures, scores, or proposals it receives.

### **Outputs**

What decision, state transition, score, classification, or escalation it produces.

### **Governing Models**

Which Phase 5B models constrain it.

### **Procedure**

The step-by-step algorithmic logic.

### **Thresholds**

Scores or conditions that determine transitions.

### **Failure Modes**

What can go wrong.

### **Governance Hooks**

When the algorithm must escalate to verification, arbitration, or constitutional review.

### **Audit Requirements**

What must be recorded.

### **Pseudocode Readiness**

A short statement identifying what is ready to be translated into pseudocode later.

## **First Engineering Observation**

The algorithms should not be treated as isolated modules.

They are interlocking regulatory procedures.

Novelty depends on Stability Budget.

Persistence depends on Grounding Score and Coherence Score.

Architectural Evolution depends on Identity Continuity and Constitutional Filtering.

Governance depends on all of them.

Therefore, Phase 6 should avoid premature modular isolation.

Each algorithm should specify its dependencies clearly.

## **Closing Compression**

Phase 5B defined the formal models of coherent intelligence.

Phase 6 defines the governed procedures by which those models become operational.

The architecture does not mature because one algorithm optimizes performance.

It matures because many algorithms regulate one another under constitutional constraint.
