## Release Center

v0.1 tests whether explicit types, governed review, enforced authority,
planned mutation, audit, rollback scope, and disciplined output can preserve
category boundaries through a complete in-process cognitive cycle.

It is an implementable architecture experiment. It is not an ARC solver,
autonomous intelligence, consciousness claim, production governance system,
or complete Phase 8 implementation.

## Included Capability

- typed symbolic structures and conservative metadata;
- typed evidence objects and immutable evidence links;
- separate memory, evidence, coherence, scale, and authority graphs;
- explicit governance, budget, and threshold state;
- enforceable reviewer registry and read-isolated dependency context;
- simplified GEA, CRA, PCA, MSSA, and CGA;
- protected IPA, SRA, NGSA, and AEA routing stubs;
- registry-validated planning and centralized working-copy application;
- typed pending escalation, decision-linked graph updates, and scoped rollback;
- exact domain delta and complete audit witnesses;
- committed-or-aborted integrated cycle with explicit fault hooks;
- output markers and finalized audit binding;
- twelve full-cycle category-collapse acceptance tests; and
- four deterministic executable examples.

## Known Limits

- In-process logical exception safety is not process-crash durability.
- Audit, memory, graph, and rollback state are runtime-only.
- Failure while abort-finalization itself is unavailable cannot yield another
  audited result.
- General semantic parsing, contradiction detection, and source retrieval are
  deferred.
- The lexical persistence/GEA scope gap remains explicitly delayed rather
  than silently repaired.
- Positive legitimacy evidence, calibrated thresholds, dynamic budgets,
  recursive escalation resolution, durable restoration, and autonomous
  self-modification are deferred.
- IPA, SRA, NGSA, and AEA do not possess their full canonical capabilities.
- External ARC tasks, adversarial controls, blinded scoring, ablation,
  latency, and cost evaluation remain future work.
