## 6. Definition of Full Module Integration

Phase 11 Full Module Integration means that all eighteen Phase 8 modules exist
as non-stub, typed, mutually compatible runtime contracts at the depth
required to operate one governed integrated cycle, preserve the accepted
category boundaries, exercise all nine governed algorithms through their
legitimate interfaces, and provide the state, IdentityKernel, governance,
escalation, rollback, audit, and deterministic test foundations required by
Phase 12. Integration completeness includes complete typed record lifecycles,
authority-closed reviewer routing, field-derived review applicability,
admissibility-gated transaction routing, logically atomic terminal
publication, and finite truthful failure boundaries.

The terminal contract requires logically atomic terminal publication.

"Full" is bounded to integration completeness. It does not mean production
completeness, unlimited autonomy, general language understanding, crash
durability, distributed commit, database recovery, continuity, functional
identity, ARC, external validation, implementation of every speculative canon
possibility, or delivery of Phase 12-15 outcomes.

Minimum conditions:

- all eighteen modules have typed, executable Phase 11 dispositions;
- no Phase 11-owned module or algorithm remains a routing-only stub;
- all nine algorithms return `ReviewDecision` through the shared interface;
- registry validation precedes mutation and mutation remains centralized;
- audit finalization is exactly once and rollback precedes high-risk change;
- escalation never becomes approval;
- later-phase work and accepted-core category boundaries remain explicit.

## 14. State and Transaction Model

Each cycle reserves one pending audit, captures an immutable baseline, clones a
working state, performs observational review, validates each decision and the
closed decision set through the registry, creates a plan, validates composition
admissibility, creates required rollback readiness, applies tentatively only
to the working copy, computes an exact domain delta, privately binds output,
audit, and CycleResult candidates, validates terminal binding, then commits or
aborts once. No reviewer mutates authoritative state. Authoritative state
changes only at `AUTHORITATIVE_TRANSACTION_COMMIT`.

## 15. IdentityKernel Boundary

Phase 11 implements a typed IdentityKernel that records invariant identifiers,
non-compensable constraints, protected dependencies, identity-impact
assessments, and comparison results used by IPA, AEA, CGA, planning, and
rollback. It is an active state and review boundary, not evidence of functional
identity, persistence across executions, consciousness, or legitimacy.

## 16. Budget and Threshold Boundary

Budgets and thresholds are visible state, named, directional, bounded, and
auditable. Phase 11 makes their effects behaviorally active and permits only
deterministic governed adjustment routes. Passing a threshold grants no
epistemic, scale, or authority status. Learned calibration and long-horizon
regulation are deferred.

## 17. Graph-Domain Boundary

MemoryGraph, EvidenceGraph, CoherenceGraph, ScaleGraph, and AuthorityGraph
remain distinct typed domains. Cross-domain references require explicit
relations and cannot transfer status implicitly. EvidenceLink remains the only
route by which typed evidence can increase grounding. Persistence does not
become evidence, coherence does not become truth, scale does not become
authority, and AuthorityGraph does not become GovernanceState.

## 18. Governance Boundary

GovernanceState makes mode, protected changes, vetoes, pending escalations,
precedent references, and authority constraints visible. CGA may approve,
reject, delay, or escalate within registry scope; escalation and review never
equal approval. Constitutional amendment and production legitimacy remain out
of scope.

## 19. Registry and Algorithm-Authority Boundary

Every algorithm has a registered identity, permitted complete-durable input
contract, readable state/graph domains, decision scope, target scale,
escalation ceiling, and prohibition on direct mutation. Projections are not
runtime inputs. Ordinary references must be backward-valid. An algorithm
cannot validate its own registry amendment, expand its authority, change the
review requirement set or plan in place, or promote a candidate by novelty,
coherence, scale, or usefulness.

`ReviewRequirementSet` derives applicable reviewers from candidate and state
fields. IdentityKernel/invariants require IPA; budget, threshold, recovery, or
high risk require SRA; evidence/grounding requires GEA; persistence, lineage,
or MemoryGraph requires PCA; coherence, tension, or CoherenceGraph requires
CRA; scale or ScaleGraph requires MSSA; architectural impact requires IPA,
SRA, and AEA; protected, constitutional, governance, or AuthorityGraph effects
require CGA; NGSA proposal validation applies only to `NGSA_PROPOSAL`.
Named scenarios are representative and nonexhaustive. Same-event requirement
mutation is forbidden: a newly exposed material requirement creates no state
effect in the current event and opens a new bounded ICC event.

The six candidate origins are `NO_ARCHITECTURAL_CANDIDATE`, `NGSA_PROPOSAL`,
`DIRECT_TYPED_ARCHITECTURAL_CANDIDATE`, `DIRECT_TYPED_PROTECTED_CHANGE`,
`EXISTING_STATE_REVISION`, and `DETERMINISTIC_TEST_FIXTURE`. Candidate origin
grants no authority. A `NoveltyCandidateProposal` contains `proposal_id`,
`source_input_refs`, `source_structure_refs`, `proposed_structure`,
`provenance_refs`, `candidate_status`, `sandbox_scope`, `budget_observations`,
`threshold_observations`, `containment_constraints`,
`permitted_review_routes`, `prohibited_uses`, `no_new_authority`,
`no_grounding_grant`, `no_persistence`, `not_active_state`, and `audit_ref`.
The final four invariants are always true. Before construction, the pending
`AuditRecord` exists; `proposal.audit_ref` points backward to that record and
never to a terminal audit, projection, decision, future placeholder, or null.
NGSA may construct or import one complete bounded proposal and then return an
observational `ReviewDecision` whose `proposal_refs` references the existing
proposal. The proposal never references its own NGSA decision, so this lineage
is one-way and acyclic. Proposal validation and per-decision validation remain
separate. A later terminal audit preserves both records' lineage without
rewriting the proposal. NGSA performs no mutation, grants no evidence,
grounding, persistence, scale, authority, planning eligibility, application
status, or achieved status, and requires no model in Phase 11.

The review DAG distinguishes `REQUIRES_PRIOR_DECISION`, `CONSUMES_PROPOSAL`,
`MAY_ESCALATE_TO`, `DOWNSTREAM_CONSUMER_OF`,
`MAY_TRIGGER_BOUNDED_SECOND_PASS`, `REGISTRY_VALIDATES`,
`PLANNER_CONSUMES_VALIDATED_DECISION`, and `SHARES_CONTEXT_ONLY`. Proposal
validation precedes proposal-conditioned review. Every applicable base
decision has its own validation instance; there is no shared executable
validation barrier. AEA consumes validated IPA/SRA and applicable domain
decisions, but no prior CGA. CGA consumes validated base and AEA decisions and
is the terminal constitutional reviewer when applicable. Per-decision, AEA,
and CGA validation remain distinct from
`DECISION_SET_CLOSURE_VALIDATION`. Closure alone grants planning eligibility,
not application authority. `CENTRAL_STATE_CHANGE_PLANNER` consumes one closed
validated set, may construct a plan, cannot broaden reviewed authority, and
must route any new domain or effect to additional review.

## 20. Escalation Boundary

Escalation is a typed unresolved transfer to a named higher review route. It
must carry source, reason, urgency, authority need, decision/audit references,
depth, and visited-route signature. Recursion is finite, loop-detected, and
testable. The exact depth policy remains pending under `P11-DEC-007`.

The eleven internal routes are IPA->CGA, SRA->CGA, NGSA->CGA, GEA->CGA,
PCA->CGA, CRA->GEA, CRA->CGA, MSSA->CGA, AEA->IPA, AEA->SRA, and AEA->CGA.
One CGA bounded-second-pass route through ICC and the ten typed external/manual
routes remain available. All are nonordering in the current event and grant no
approval. ICC opens a new bounded event that preserves depth, visited-route
signature, and parent audit lineage and repeats validation and closure gates.

## 21. Rollback Boundary

`RollbackReadinessReservation` and `RollbackReadinessRecord` are separate.
High-risk order is `CENTRAL_STATE_CHANGE_PLANNER` ->
`ROLLBACK_READINESS_RESERVATION` ->
`COMPOSITION_ADMISSIBILITY_VALIDATION` -> `ROLLBACK_READINESS` ->
`CENTRAL_WORKING_STATE_APPLICATION`. Reservation grants neither readiness nor
authority; completed readiness exists only after success. Unused reservation
is cancelled and attempted completion consumes it. Low-risk and
no-application routes create neither record. High-risk failure uses the exact
same-attempt readiness record for restoration; low-risk failure discards the
working copy. Baseline equivalence is required before aborted-result
validation. Recovery is not approval.

## 22. Audit Boundary

Every completed logical cycle owns exactly one terminal audit that ends as
`COMMITTED` or `ABORTED`. Audit candidates remain private until authoritative
commit. A terminalization-failed cycle has no committed terminal audit and no
normal CycleResult. Audit binds baseline, inputs, reviews, registry validation,
plan, recovery, effects, output, unresolved items, escalation, and terminal
reason. Durable storage, search, signatures, and crash recovery are not
claimed.

## 23. Output and CycleResult Boundary

CycleResult must bind final state, audit, exact delta, output or explicit
absence, unresolved items, escalation, monitoring, and errors. OutputObject
must preserve support, grounding/uncertainty, epistemic markers, tensions,
authority, and finalized audit linkage. Returned content is not truth merely
because it is coherent, audited, or authorized.

`DURABLE_RECORD` and `REPRESENTATIVE_PROJECTION` are distinct. Projections are
non-authoritative, non-persistable, non-referenceable, not runtime inputs, and
cannot satisfy complete durable contracts; each names its projected durable
type and exact omissions. This applies to `ReviewRequirementSetProjection`,
`CompositionAdmissibilityProjection`, and `TerminalResultProjection`.
Ordinary `_ref` values point backward only to existing durable records.
Future completion may use an existing typed `_reservation_ref`; conditional
null is allowed only by a declared lifecycle rule. No future completed record
may be fabricated.
